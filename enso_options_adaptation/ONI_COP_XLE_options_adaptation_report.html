Option implementations of the strong-but-cooling ONI trade

The underlying strategy called for long XLE and short COP for 40 trading days after delayed ONI remained at least 1.0 but started declining. This report freezes the same two 2024 signals and compares five defined-risk option structures.

Result

O2 Long COP ATM put ranked first. It generated $13,245 before tax and after modeled trading friction, or 13.25% of initial portfolio capital. The 37% short-term tax scenario reduces that to $8,344, or 8.34%. At most 10% of current portfolio equity was allocated to option debit at each signal.

Liquidity warning. The winning COP puts had reported exit-day volume of only 8 and 2 contracts. The closing trade marks may not represent executable prices for the simulated 23-contract exits, even after the 2.5% price haircut.

Contract selection

Contracts were queried with Massive's historical as_of parameter at each entry. Expiry had to be at least seven calendar days beyond the planned exit and closest to 75 days from entry. The at-the-money contract minimizes strike distance to the unadjusted underlying opening price. The vertical wing targets approximately 10% out of the money. Every selected contract has an option trade bar on both the exact entry and exit date.

Execution and tax assumptions

  • Entry uses the option's first reported trade-bar open on the signal date. Exit uses the same contract's reported close on the planned 40th trading day.
  • Each option purchase is marked 2.5% above the trade price and each sale 2.5% below it. Short spread legs receive 2.5% less on entry and cost 2.5% more to close.
  • Commission is $0.65 per contract per leg at both entry and exit.
  • Contract quantity is the largest whole number whose modeled entry debit remains inside its assigned 10% risk budget.
  • The base tax scenario applies 37% to positive aggregate 2024 short-term profit after friction. Losses offset gains inside the test year. State tax, net-investment-income tax, loss carryforwards and cash yield are excluded.

What the option result measures

The test measures realized option-price P&L, not signal quality. Signal quality was established by the earlier walk-forward COP-minus-XLE return calculation. Option P&L adds strike selection, expiry, convexity, time decay, volatility pricing and liquidity. A correct underlying direction can still lose money in a long option if the move is too small or arrives too late.

Limitations

  • Massive daily option aggregates contain qualifying trades, not historical bid-ask quotes. The 2.5% execution haircut is a scenario assumption and cannot reconstruct the actual spread.
  • Historical implied volatility and Greeks were not available from the daily-bar files and were not backfilled from current snapshots.
  • Daily option closes can reflect trades at different times. Requiring exact entry and exit bars reduces stale-mark risk but does not synchronize every leg.
  • The two signals occurred in the same 2023–24 El Niño episode and the same energy uptrend/low-volatility regime.
  • The best structure was selected from five candidates after observing both trades.
