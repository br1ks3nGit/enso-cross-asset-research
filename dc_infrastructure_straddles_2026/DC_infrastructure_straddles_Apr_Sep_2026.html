Capital-constrained results

Returns include USD/HKD conversion at each entry and exit, 2.5% option-price slippage on every transaction, and USD 0.65 commission per contract, per leg, per side. Base-case tax is zero because Hong Kong generally does not tax capital gains; classification as taxable trading income is facts-dependent and is not resolved by this model.

GEV's −3.04% is not evidence that it was the best straddle. Four of five candidate GEV trades were unaffordable and therefore contributed zero. Among names with all five available trades executed, VRT lost 28.59% and ETN lost 63.04%.

Exact formulas and metric definitions

  • Raw entry premium (USD) = 100 × (call open + put open).
  • All-in entry debit (USD) = raw entry premium × (1 + 2.5%) + 2 × USD 0.65.
  • Exit proceeds (USD) = max[100 × (call close + put close) × (1 − 2.5%) − 2 × USD 0.65, 0].
  • Net P&L (HKD) = exit proceeds USD × exit USD/HKD − entry debit USD × entry USD/HKD.
  • Return on debit = net P&L HKD ÷ entry debit HKD.
  • Portfolio return = (ending HKD equity ÷ HKD 100,000) − 1. Skipped trades contribute zero.
  • Premium/spot = (call open + put open) ÷ stock open. It approximates the expiry move needed to cover premium before costs.
  • Absolute stock move = |stock exit close ÷ stock entry open − 1|.
  • Magnitude coverage = absolute stock move ÷ premium/spot. Above 1.0 means the underlying move exceeded that simple premium ratio; it does not guarantee option profit before expiry.
  • Win rate = count(net straddle P&L > 0) ÷ observed straddles.

Method and limitations

  1. Entry is the first common U.S. trading session of each month from April through September 2026.
  2. At entry, query Massive’s contract reference endpoint with that date in as_of; select a common call/put expiry 45–70 days out, closest to 55 DTE, then the same strike nearest the stock open.
  3. Require an exact option trade bar for both legs on entry and exit. Exit is the twentieth trading session including entry; September stops on the latest available date.
  4. No bid/ask quotes were available in this dataset. The 2.5% per-transaction haircut is a sensitivity assumption, not a reconstruction of the NBBO.
  5. Option opens/closes can be stale or unrepresentative in thin contracts. Corporate events, early exercise, dividends and tax-lot rules are not modeled.
  6. The company universe was economically selected before return ranking. It is still a small, non-random sample with substantial multiple-testing risk.
