What each control does

Concentration cap + inverse volatility

Weights are proportional to the inverse of each stock's trailing 12-month volatility, then capped at 25%. This limits a volatile stock's risk contribution and prevents one name from exceeding one quarter of long capital.

20% volatility target

Gross exposure is the smaller of 100% and 20% divided by the annualized volatility of the prior 12 shadow-core returns. It de-risks after observed volatility rises and never adds leverage after calm periods.

XLI trend rule

Exposure is 100% when prior-month XLI is above its trailing six-month mean and its trailing three-month return is positive; otherwise it is 50%. All inputs precede the test month.

Drawdown brake

The unscaled inverse-volatility shadow portfolio determines the state: 100% above −10%, 50% from −10% to −20%, and 25% below −20%. A shadow portfolio permits automatic re-entry when the underlying basket recovers.

Expected-shortfall budget

The mean return in the worst 10% of the prior 24 months estimates tail loss. Exposure is capped so this estimate does not exceed 8% in one month. The 10% estimator is used for sizing; the report separately shows 95% realized expected shortfall.

Regime-dependent XLI hedge

The combined core shorts 25% of estimated stock beta in a positive XLI regime and 50% in a weak regime. Betas use up to 24 prior months and are clipped to 0.25–2.00 per stock to avoid unstable hedge sizes.

Combination rule

The combined core uses the minimum of the volatility, expected-shortfall, drawdown and trend multipliers. It does not multiply them, because multiplication can unintentionally drive a diversified portfolio close to cash. The core–signal portfolio places 85% in that combined core and 15% in the existing confirmed ENSO sleeve.

Metric definitions

  • Total return: Π(1 + rₜ) − 1. CAGR: [Π(1 + rₜ)]12/N − 1.
  • Annualized volatility: sample standard deviation of monthly returns × √12. Sharpe: mean monthly return / monthly standard deviation × √12. A zero cash rate is used.
  • Sortino: mean monthly return / standard deviation of negative monthly returns × √12. The target return is zero.
  • Maximum drawdown: minimum of wealthₜ / prior peak wealthₜ − 1. Calmar: CAGR / absolute maximum drawdown.
  • Monthly 95% VaR: the positive magnitude of the empirical 5th-percentile monthly return. Monthly 95% expected shortfall: the positive magnitude of the mean return at or below that percentile. Both are historical and based on only 63 months.
  • XLI beta: Cov(rportfolio, rXLI) / Var(rXLI). Up/down capture: mean portfolio return divided by mean XLI return in XLI-positive/negative months.
  • CAGR retained: portfolio CAGR / basic-basket CAGR. Drawdown reduced: 1 − |portfolio max drawdown| / |basic max drawdown|.
  • Turnover: sum of absolute monthly changes in all long and hedge weights, annualized. Cost drag: turnover × 15 bp, annualized; it excludes taxes, bid–ask slippage and financing.

Implementation and limitations

  1. Rebalance monthly after the information date. Calculate every trailing statistic through the previous month.
  2. Use adjusted Massive bars and verify splits/dividends. Do not substitute unadjusted prices.
  3. Keep a 25% name limit, 100% gross-long limit and the fixed 85/15 strategic allocation. Do not raise risk to recover losses.
  4. Monitor model/data failure separately: a missing price, stale ONI release, or unavailable news feed should block a new signal, not be imputed as bullish.
  5. Tax is excluded because Hong Kong tax treatment depends on facts, residence and whether activity is considered a trade. Confirm with a tax professional. Borrow costs and short-sale constraints on XLI are also excluded.
